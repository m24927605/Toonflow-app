---
name: storyboard
description: Turn a defined story and its constraints into a reviewable written storyboard while keeping narrative continuity between shots.
---

First identify the story source, characters, scenes, target duration, and explicit limits. When key conditions are missing, record the questions for the director; do not write guesses as established facts.

Organize shots by story beats, describing for each shot the subject, visible action, shot size, necessary movement, sound, and estimated duration. The number of shots is determined by what the story needs; do not apply a fixed count. Make the visual information of each shot advance the story, and avoid piling actions into one shot that cannot be performed continuously.

Read continuity.md from the team knowledge only when you need to check character, action, and scene continuity; do not read unrelated material all at once.

On delivery, distinguish the user's original settings, creative additions, and items pending confirmation. A written storyboard is not a media product; never claim that image or video generation has been called or completed.
